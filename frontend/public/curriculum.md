**Technical AI Safety Fellowship**

_Summer 2026, 8 Week Curriculum_

The curriculum is divided into two sections. Below, I share the ideal outcomes I have in mind

- **Weeks 1–4**: Fellows have engaged with and understand, in their own terms, the arguments for and against existential risk arising from advanced AI in the near future.

- **Weeks 5–8**: Fellows have a broad understanding of the different types of technical research being done to mitigate these risks, and they have concrete plans for upskilling and pursuing research or contributing to AI safety in another manner.

**Overview**

This week is _optional_ and completed _asynchronously_. The goal is to ensure that fellows have a basic technical understanding of machine learning, which will help them grasp some of the research we cover in the fellowship. **Week 1: Superintelligence and current trajectory**

**Overview**

This week grounds participants in the empirical basis for the near-term development of advanced AI. The readings cover the current state of the frontier of AI models, the scaling trends that inform near-term capability forecasts, and the argument that once AI can accelerate AI research, the path to superintelligence may be shorter than intuition suggests. 

**Learning Objectives**

By the end of week 1, fellows should be able to:

- Interpret empirical scaling trends and benchmarks as evidence about trajectories.

**Learning Objectives**

**Week 5: Scalable oversight and automated alignment**

**Overview**

This week covers one of the main suggested approaches to aligning a superintelligence: handing off the problem to models more capable than ourselves. Scalable oversight asks how humans can train and evaluate models that outstrip them, with weak-to-strong generalization as one empirical handle. Automated alignment asks whether we can hand the alignment problem itself to AI, and why delegating it may be harder than it sounds.

**Learning Objectives**

By the end of week 5, fellows should be able to:

- Explain scalable oversight and weak-to-strong generalization.

- Describe the case for automating alignment research and its appeal.

- Explain cases where automated alignment may fail and potential mitigations.

**Week 6: Control**

**Overview**

This week covers one approach to safety that does not require solving alignment outright. Control asks how we can extract useful work from powerful AIs, limit the damage they can do, and incriminate them, even assuming they may be misaligned and trying to subvert us.

**Learning Objectives**

By the end of week 6, fellows should be able to:

- Explain the case for AI control and how it differs from alignment as a safety strategy.

- Understand the empirical methodologies used in initial AI control work.

**Week 7: Mechanistic interpretability and evals**

**Overview**

This week introduces two of the most important empirical approaches to understanding what frontier models are doing under the hood: mechanistic interpretability and evaluations. The week emphasizes how these two threads are converging into alignment auditing: using interpretability tools to check whether a model has the goals it appears to have.

**Learning Objectives**

By the end of week 7, fellows should be able to:

- Describe, at a high level, attribution graphs and linear probes and their uses 

- Point to at least one concrete finding from modern interpretability on a frontier model 

- Distinguish different types of evals, e.g., capability, propensity, alignment audits
